Ten layouts for the Orbit screen

The Orbit screen is the Logi Action Ring page. A gray canvas holds the ring. One card on the right is either the settings or Add to orbit, and it has to be swapped to see the other. A bar along the bottom carries the title, How to, Add item, and Reset.

Each layout below keeps the same controls — item size, item shape, orbit radius, hot corner, hover style, and the add catalog — and moves where the preview, the catalog, and the settings sit.

Three columns

Settings, the orbit, and Add are open together. The right card no longer has to be one thing or the other. The orbit gets the middle, and it is smaller than the canvas you have now.

You can see. A size change and the catalog at the same time, against the live ring.

Gives up. The large preview. A ring this size is for checking, not for dragging across a big canvas.

Stage and benches

The orbit is a stage across the top. Settings and Add split the bench under it. The side card and the bottom title bar are both gone. The title sits in the window bar.

You can see. The whole ring, then both jobs underneath it, in one downward read.

Gives up. Side-by-side comparison of a setting and the exact bubble it changes. The controls are below the thing they affect.

On the geometry

Each setting sits on the thing it changes. Item size is on a bubble, radius is a handle on the ring, shape sits under the hub, and the hot corner is the mark in the corner of the canvas. Add is a tray along the bottom.

You can see. The consequence in place. Dragging the handle is the radius.

Gives up. A quiet canvas. The preview is also the form, so a first visit has to learn which mark is a control.

Library

Add is the page. Apps, folders, and links are a grid you tick onto the orbit. The orbit shrinks to a live summary in the header, and the five settings become one strip beside it.

You can see. Everything you could add, and whether it is already on the orbit.

Gives up. Dragging to a slot. The ring is a receipt, not the surface you arrange.

Canvas and sheet

The orbit is the whole page. Settings and Add share a sheet along the bottom, both open. The sheet is the only chrome. The centered Orbit Layout bar is gone.

You can see. A large ring, with both jobs parked where your hands already are.

Gives up. Height. The sheet covers the lower part of the canvas, so the ring sits high.

Playlist

Order is a list: grip, icon, name, kind. Rearranging is dragging a row. The ring on the right is a preview of that list. Settings sit under the list. Add is the open section at the bottom of it.

You can see. The sequence, the kind of each item, and the ring that sequence produces.

Gives up. Editing on the ring. Slots stop being the drag target.

Shelves

Settings are one shelf across the top. Add is a second shelf, a sideways row of apps and folders. The orbit gets everything under them. There is no card and no sidebar.

You can see. Every frequent control without scrolling, and the ring at full width.

Gives up. Room for the long catalog. Shortcuts, groups, and tools have to scroll sideways.

Summon desk

The preview is drawn where the hot corner will open it, in the corner of a monitor. Settings sit in the opposite corner so they do not cover it. Add is a tray along the bottom edge of that monitor.

You can see. The orbit in the place and the shape it will actually have.

Gives up. A centered medallion. A center summon and a corner summon want different previews, so this page has to follow the corner you picked.

Two cards

The screen uses Home’s two cards. Arrange holds the orbit and the five settings. Stock holds Add. The page stops being a canvas beside a sidebar.

You can see. The two jobs as peers, in the same language as the rest of SwiftX.

Gives up. One continuous canvas. Dragging from Stock onto Arrange crosses a card edge.

One job

An index lists Size, Shape, Radius, Corner, Hover, and Add. The orbit stays put. Only the job you picked is open, large enough to use without a scrolling side card. Radius is the one open here. Add, when picked, replaces that panel with the catalog.

You can see. One decision, clearly, against the ring.

Gives up. The overview. You cannot scan item size and the catalog in the same glance.
