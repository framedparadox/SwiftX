Ten ways to draw the dock

The dock you can summon today is eight white circles on an even ring around the petal mark. The first six are the stock set — Notepad, GitHub, Downloads, Tools, Calculator, File Explorer — and the last two are always Settings and Close. Tools opens a second ring outside the first. A name appears only while the pointer is on a circle.

That is the Logitech Options+ action ring: the same eight places, the same outward name pills, the same concentric flyout. The Orbit page in the app is that ring’s settings screen. It is a strong memory once the angles are learned, and a slow first read. Close occupies an angle a launcher could use, a group is only a short arc, and a hot corner has to switch to a different shape.

Each stage below uses that same set and the same rules. A leaf launches and the dock goes away. Tools opens where it stands. Escape steps back. The hub dismisses. Click Tools on any stage.

Ring, as shipped

Equal bubbles, forty-five degrees apart, slot zero straight up. The hub is the mark, and clicking it only closes. Hover paints the name in a pill along the same ray. Tools keeps the inner ring still and adds an outer one, so the angles you already know stay put.

Keeps. A stable place for each item, a flyout that doesn’t shove the parent aside, and a hub that means “back” or “done”.

Gives up. A name you can read before you point, a Close that isn’t the same size as Notepad, and a shape that still fits when the summon point is a screen corner.

Wedges

The live orbit already treats a click in the gap as a click on the nearest item. Here that area is the control. The name sits in the slice, so the dock can be read at a glance. Tools replaces the disc with its own slices. Close darkens its slice under the pointer and draws its mark inward.

Keeps. One gesture from the center, the whole disc as a target, and a hub that still means back.

Gives up. The quiet desktop. A full disc covers more wallpaper than eight bubbles, and long names get tight at the inner edge of a slice.

Pointer fan

The dock is rooted at the pointer and opens into the free part of the screen. That is the room a hot corner and a cursor summon actually have. Names sit on the ray, past the icon, on two steps so neighbours clear each other. Tools opens a list in the free corner, and the fan of icons stays so you can still see where you were.

Keeps. A short flick from where the pointer already is, and a flyout that doesn’t wrap around the whole set.

Gives up. A dock that looks the same wherever it was summoned. The fan has to face the open side of the screen, so the angles change with the corner.

Reading strip

One horizontal bar. The name sits beside the icon, and you read it left to right. Settings and Close stand past a fence at the end of the bar. Tools hangs a tray from its own chip, joined to it by a short stem, and the rest of the bar does not move.

Keeps. Every name visible, Close fenced off from the things you launch, and a group that opens without a second geometry.

Gives up. Directional memory. After a few dozen launches, a ring or a fan is faster than re-reading a bar. The bar is also wide.

Petal board

The mark is the layout. Four petals are the four large targets — Notepad, File Explorer, Downloads, Tools — and GitHub, Calculator, Settings, and Close sit as small chips around the cross. Tools opens as a list inside its own petal. The hub in the cross is still the way back.

Keeps. The SwiftX mark as something you aim at, a clear difference between a primary and the rest, and Close off the main targets.

Gives up. A free arrangement of eight equals. The petals imply four primaries, so a fifth app you use all day has to live on a chip or push someone off a petal.

Compass

Four arms. The item at the end of an arm is the one you came for; a second item on that arm is smaller and closer to the hub. Tools lengthens the west arm into a stack. Close sits under the hub, on none of the arms, and shrinks under the pointer.

Keeps. Muscle memory in the easiest directions — up, down, left, right — and a visible rank between a primary and its neighbour.

Gives up. Room for eight peers. Two items have to share an arm, and the stack from Tools has to pick a direction that stays on screen.

Magnifying shelf

A bar along the bottom of the summon. The item under the pointer grows, and its name appears above it. Tools rises as a stack from that icon. Close does the opposite of everyone else: as the pointer approaches, it shrinks, so Quit never becomes the easy target.

Keeps. A gesture people already know from a desktop dock, names for the item you are about to hit, and Close pulled back from the magnification.

Gives up. The orbit. This is a dock in the ordinary sense, and it wants the edge of the screen more than it wants the cursor.

Focus dial

One item is written out: icon, name, and what it is. The others wait as beads. Point at a bead, or use the arrow keys, and the center follows. The big target launches the item in focus. Tools swaps the beads for its contents and a bead that returns to the orbit.

Keeps. A full name and a kind before you commit, and a dock that stays the same size however many items you add.

Gives up. Seeing the whole set at once. Launching is a scrub, then a confirm. That is slower when you already know you wanted Notepad.

Hex nest

Cells share edges. There is no gap to interpret and no circle implied. The center cell is the hub. The six user items are the first ring. Settings and Close sit on the next ring, darker, still on the grid. Tools swaps the first ring for its own cells and a cell that returns.

Keeps. A neighbour for every item, a center that means back, and a way to set system actions one step further out.

Gives up. Long names. A hex is a small page, and File Explorer is already pressing the edges. A group of eight children would need a second ring again.

Priority spiral

Distance is priority. Notepad sits against the hub; each next item stands further along one path; Close is the last and the smallest. The line is the order. Tools buds its children at that point on the path, so a group is a knot in the line.

Keeps. A single reading order, a visible difference between daily and rare, and a flyout that doesn’t wrap a circle around everything.

Gives up. Equal reach. The far items take a longer move, which is the point, and the path gets crowded if two groups open at once. Only one group opens here.

Summoned list

A menu at the pointer. Each row has a number key, the icon, the name, and the kind. Tools expands under its own row. The mark at the top is the hub: it closes the group, then the dock. This is the control case for the other nine.

Keeps. The fastest cold read on the page, keyboard access without memorising angles, and Close on its own row at the end.

Gives up. The spatial memory the orbit is for. Once you know the set, a flick beats a scan. If a spatial dock can’t beat this list after a week of use, the spatial dock is only a picture.
